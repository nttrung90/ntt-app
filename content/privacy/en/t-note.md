# T-Note Privacy Policy

**Last updated:** November 11, 2026
**Effective from:** November 11, 2026

T-Note is a cross-platform notes application (Android, iOS and Windows) built with an offline-first architecture. It can sync your notes via Google Firebase and back them up to your personal Google Drive.

This policy explains what data we collect, why, where it is stored, who it is shared with, and what rights you have. By downloading, installing or using T-Note, you confirm that you have read and understood this policy.

> **Quick summary**
> - Your notes are stored on your device and only synced to Firebase when you sign in.
> - You can back up your notes to a private folder in your own Google Drive.
> - The app **does not sell**, **does not rent**, **does not use data for advertising**, and contains **no ad SDKs**.
> - The app **does not request** access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone, messages, or your entire device storage.
> - You can delete notes, delete backups, and request account deletion at any time.

## 2. Data we collect

### 2.1. Note content you create

When you use T-Note, the app processes the following data to provide the note-taking features:

- Note titles and content.
- To-do checklists, including task text and completion status.
- Tags/hashtags you attach to notes.
- Background color, pin status, archive status and trash status.
- Note creation and update times.

Note content is entered by you. You are not required to provide any information, but without entering content the app cannot perform its note-taking function.

### 2.2. Account data

When you sign in, Firebase Authentication may process:

- Email address.
- Display name and profile photo URL (if you sign in with Google).
- A user identifier (UID) generated by Firebase.
- Sign-in method (Google, email/password, or Guest/Anonymous).

If you use Guest mode, Firebase creates an anonymous UID and does not require an email, name or photo. If you register with email/password, your password is handled by Firebase Authentication; the app does not store, display, or have access to your password.

### 2.3. Sync and backup data

- **Firebase sync:** When you sign in, notes are stored in Cloud Firestore at the path `users/{UID}/notes/{noteId}`. Firestore security rules only allow the account owner to read and write their own data.
- **Google Drive backup:** When you actively tap backup and grant permission, the app creates a JSON file containing all your notes plus some metadata (file name, creation time, note count) in the `T-Note Backups` folder in your Google Drive.
- **Drive permission scope:** The app only requests the scope `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file`. This scope only allows the app to create, read, modify and delete files that the app itself created or that you actively open with the app. T-Note **cannot** access other files in your Google Drive.

### 2.4. Data stored locally on your device

The app stores the following in its private app storage (SharedPreferences):

- Your list of notes as JSON, so you can use the app offline.
- A list of note IDs pending sync to Firebase.
- Your Light/Dark/System theme preference.

Local data resides in the OS-isolated storage area of the app. The app does not request access to your entire device storage.

### 2.5. Technical data processed by Google services

To operate the service, Google (Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Google Sign-In, Google Drive API and Google Fonts) may process technical data such as:

- IP address.
- Device type, operating system and app version.
- Authentication tokens and session information.
- Access times, error logs and security events.

The app may load the Inter font from Google Fonts servers at runtime; this request may send your IP address and minimal device information to Google. We do not use this technical data for advertising or sell it to third parties.

### 2.6. Data we do NOT collect

T-Note does not request and does not knowingly collect:

- Precise or approximate location.
- Contacts, call logs, SMS messages.
- Camera, microphone, photos or files in device storage.
- Health data or biometric data.
- Advertising ID or data used to track you across apps.
- Payment information.

The app integrates no ad SDKs, displays no ads, and does not sell personal data.

## 4. Data storage and protection

### 4.1. Where data is stored

- **On your device:** notes and theme preferences are stored locally so the app works offline.
- **Cloud Firestore:** when signed in, data is stored on Google Cloud infrastructure in the region determined by the Firebase project configuration.
- **Google Drive:** backups are stored in your own Google Drive account; we have no access to your Drive beyond files created by the app.

### 4.2. Protection measures

- Data in transit between the app and Google is protected by TLS encryption provided by Google's official SDKs.
- Firebase Authentication manages sign-in sessions and tokens.
- Cloud Firestore security rules limit each user to reading/writing only their own data:

```
match /users/{userId}/notes/{noteId} {
  allow read, write: if request.auth != null && request.auth.uid == userId;
}
```

- Google Drive is accessed only through the `drive.file` scope, not your entire Drive.
- Local data resides in the app's private storage protected by the operating system.

**About encryption:** The app does not separately encrypt note content at the application level. Local data relies on the operating system's isolation and protection mechanisms; data on Firebase and backup files on Google Drive are encrypted by Google in transit and at rest under Google's infrastructure. Backup JSON files are not additionally encrypted by the app, so you should protect your Google account.

However, no security method is absolutely safe. You should lock your device with a PIN/password or biometrics, protect your Google account, and not share your device with untrusted people. If a note contains someone else's sensitive information, you need their consent or another appropriate legal basis before entering it.

## 6. Retention periods

- **Notes on device:** until you delete the note, clear app data, or uninstall the app.
- **Notes in trash:** until you permanently delete them or empty the trash.
- **Notes in Cloud Firestore:** until you delete the note or your account; then removed from live systems within a maximum of 30 days, unless the law requires longer retention.
- **Backups on Google Drive:** until you delete the backup or the file in Drive; we do not delete them automatically.
- **Firebase account information:** until you request account deletion; processed within a maximum of 30 days, unless the law requires otherwise.
- **Google technical logs:** according to Google/Firebase retention policies.

**Note:** Uninstalling the app or clearing app data only deletes local data. Synced notes may still exist in Cloud Firestore and will be downloaded again when you sign back in. To delete everything, delete your notes while signed in or request account deletion.

## 7. Deleting data and accounts

### 7.1. Deleting notes

- Move notes to the trash in the app.
- Open Trash and permanently delete individual notes or empty the entire trash.
- When signed in, deletions sync to Cloud Firestore.

### 7.2. Deleting Google Drive backups

- Open **Settings → Back up to Google Drive**.
- Select the backup to delete and confirm deletion.
- You can also delete the `T-Note Backups` folder directly in Google Drive.

### 7.3. Deleting your account

Currently, to delete your entire account and related data, please email [firstntt90@gmail.com](mailto:firstntt90@gmail.com) with the subject **"T-Note account deletion request"**, from the email address you registered with (if any). Including the UID shown in the account settings (if available) will speed up verification.

We will:

1. Verify your request within 7 business days.
2. Delete your Firebase Authentication account and your note data in Cloud Firestore.
3. Notify you upon completion, within a maximum of 30 days from successful verification.

Backups on Google Drive are in your own account, so you need to delete them yourself; if you cannot, let us know in your request email for guidance. Some technical logs retained by Google may persist longer under Google's policies and legal obligations.
